Your Relationship Coach

The Dance

Why one of you reaches, and one of you retreats. The most common pattern couples bring to coaching.

Does this sound familiar?

Something small sets it off. One of you wants to sort it out right now, and follows the other into the kitchen, still explaining.

The other goes quiet, finds something that suddenly needs doing, and leaves the room.

An hour later, the silence is louder than the argument was.

Two people, two instincts

When you feel disconnected,
you protect yourselves in opposite directions

One of youmoves towards
→ the dance happens here ←
One of youmoves away

Neither is wrong, and either of you can be either circle. The roles can even swap depending on the topic.

Pursuermoves towards

The Pursuer

cannot relax while something is unresolved
  • Wants to talk about it now, fix it now
  • Asks questions, seeks reassurance, follows
  • Reaching out is their repair attempt, even when it comes out sharp
“If I stop reaching, we will drift apart.”
Avoidermoves away

The Avoider

feels flooded when things get intense
  • Goes quiet, needs space, lets things settle
  • Looks calm outside, overwhelmed inside
  • Withdrawing is how they keep the peace and avoid making it worse
“If I engage, I will get it wrong and make it worse.”

reaches, pushes, asks again

goes quiet and pulls away

Same dance, different names

The Hailstorm

gets louder and bigger

The Turtle

pulls deeper into the shell

Harville Hendrix (Imago) calls it the hailstorm and the turtle. Sue Johnson (EFT) calls it pursue and withdraw. Different researchers, same dance.

The part to hold onto
It is not your characters. It is a cycle.

You are not a nag, and your partner is not cold. Those are the roles the dance hands you. And a cycle can be changed.

Your strengths in the dance

Where your strengths live changes how you dance

Executing

“Fix it now” energy can fuel the chase. Unresolved feels unbearable.

Influencing

Needing to be heard can turn the volume up when it matters most.

Relationship Building

Feels the disconnection first. The radar that starts the reach.

Strategic Thinking

Retreats to think it through. The pause that can read as hiding.

None of this is a flaw. It is your talent under pressure.

Your strengths on the way out

The same strengths walk you out

Executing

Keep the return-time promise. Reliability rebuilds safety.

Influencing

Be the one who names the dance out loud, warmly.

Relationship Building

Make the first repair bid. The text, the hand, the cup of tea.

Strategic Thinking

Design the pause that suits you both. When, how long, how you come back.

Aimed at the cycle instead of each other, your strengths become the way out.
