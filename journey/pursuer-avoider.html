The two steps of the dance

The Pursuer

moves towards connection

  • Wants to talk about it now
  • Asks, follows, seeks reassurance
  • Reaching out is their repair attempt
Core fear: “If I stop reaching, we will drift apart.”

The Avoider

moves away to self-protect

  • Goes quiet, needs space
  • Waits for things to settle
  • Withdrawing is how they keep the peace
Core fear: “If I engage, I will make it worse.”

Same dance, different names. Harville Hendrix, who created Imago therapy, calls this pair the hailstorm and the turtle. The hailstorm gets louder and bigger; the turtle pulls deeper into its shell. Other researchers call it pursue and withdraw. Whatever the label, it is the most common pattern couples bring to coaching, and it is workable.

The part to hold onto

It is not your characters. It is a cycle. The pursuing feels like a storm, so the turtle hides. The hiding feels like abandonment, so the storm grows. Each of you is protecting yourself, and each move sets off the other.

“I think we are in our dance right now. Can we slow down?”

Stepping out of the dance

1

Name it when you see it. Either of you can call it, gently: “I think we are in our dance right now.”

2

The turtle says when. Space is allowed. Coming out of the shell just enough to say when you will be back makes the space safe instead of frightening.

3

The storm softens. Easing off enough to let your partner take that space, trusting they will return, is what lets the turtle come back out.

This week

You do not have to fix the dance yet. Just notice it, and notice which step is yours. Noticing is the first step out.
